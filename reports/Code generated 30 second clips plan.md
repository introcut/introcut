# Draw Flat 2D Shorts Before 3D

**The best realistic plan is to build the 30-second clip as a code-drawn flat-2D story engine in four tiers, ship the first two tiers in about 6 to 9 weeks, and treat 3D as an optional, gated bet, because the ZackD.Films look cannot be reached by code alone.** The research found that ZackD.Films (29.3M subscribers) is staffed by contract Blender animators, so its look comes from authored 3D art, lighting and simulation ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@zackdfilms/), [recruiting page](https://zackdfilms.recruitee.com/o/3d-generalist)). Code can reproduce its structure (narrator, escalation, twist), its cut and camera rhythm, its squash-and-stretch pops and its sound design, but not its character appeal or materials. Tier 0 and 1 (text, numbers, charts, chat, UI mockups, user stills with camera moves) reuse almost everything already built and reach "polished template video". Tier 2 (a flat-vector cast driven by a closed acting vocabulary, our own puppet rig) is the first tier where characters act out a story, and it reaches "stick-figure/explainer-channel grade", not film. Tier 3 (three.js toon characters) costs 25 to 40 days (estimate) and, with free CC0 characters, would read as game assets. The AI never writes code: it writes a beat sheet, then small per-scene cards from a closed catalogue, which Zod, code checks and a deterministic compiler turn into a timeline for the existing `renderFrame`. The two things that decide success are unmeasured today: how fast a character scene renders and exports on a weak PC, and whether a free or small model fills scene cards validly. Both get early gates. Owners must decide on the character art source, the Kokoro/eSpeak GPL question, the Supertonic OpenRAIL-M voice, CC-BY music and the Quaternius licence conflict (section 5).

## The ZackD look is Blender-made, so code should copy the structure and own a different look

Evidence first. ZackD.Films shows about 29.3M subscribers, 80.75B views and 4,104 videos as of 20 Oct 2026, with top titles that are true-story disasters and corporate blunders ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@zackdfilms/)). Its own recruiting page asks contract 3D animators for 20-45 s, 9:16 clips, lists character animation, camera work, lookdev, shader effects, simulations and VFX, calls Blender a major plus, and pays a flat rate per project ([ZackD recruiting](https://zackdfilms.recruitee.com/o/3d-generalist)). Freelancers selling the style describe sweeping camera moves, slow-motion beats, dramatic lighting and physics simulations, which is seller marketing rather than channel documentation ([Upwork listing](https://www.upwork.com/services/product/video-audio-zack-d-filmsstyle-cinematic-short-blender-3d-animation-simulations-1983070890520437486)). Imitator tutorials use AI images, an image-to-video tool and CapCut, again not code ([Lilys](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/ai-shorts-20251110/make-zack-d-style-ai-shorts-free)). No researcher found any channel at ZackD scale made purely by code, and none found a creator breakdown of the channel's real pipeline, so the Blender claim rests on the recruiting page and listings. The code-made precedents are in other genres: Anthropic's Claude Motion pitches "30-second clips from business data" using code-animated text, charts, shapes and images with no video model, though only secondary summaries describe it ([Layer3 Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/claude-motion)), and a code-versus-pixel study found pixel video models scored near zero on text-heavy explainers where code methods won ([Code2Video](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)).

What follows is the researchers' own judgment, not data. Of the nine factors that make the channel read as premium, code approximates five well: squash, stretch and overshoot (spring easing), camera moves with cuts every 1-3 s, pacing and script (the LLM's job), sound design (whooshes, impacts, ducking), and cheap finishing (vignette, grain, mild glow). It approximates three partially: character design (the biggest gap, because free packs are generic), walk and emote cycles, and lighting (toon materials, rim light, gradient backdrops). It approximates one poorly: debris, fluid and cloth simulation. The researchers' minimum subset that would still look intentional is a fixed palette and 3-6 backdrops, a small cast with 5-8 poses, a pop on every beat, a camera rig with cuts and whip transitions, light finishing, strong sound and kinetic captions, and they judge that dropping the pops, the camera or the sound makes the result look cheap faster than dropping the finishing. None of that has been tested on audiences.

The honest ceiling per tier follows. Effort figures are researcher estimates for one developer with an AI assistant, and the quality statements are judgment.

| Tier | What it is | Realistic quality ceiling | Closeness to ZackD | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (built) | Hook text, big number, end card, stills with push-in, word captions | Clean card video with voice; looks like a good template | Structure only | done |
| 1 | Add chart, steps, bullets, chat story, screen demo, motion comic (user stills + parallax, bubbles), better camera, sound | "Polished Canva/CapCut-grade explainer"; image quality is set by what the user supplies | Pacing, hook, twist, sound; no characters | 12-19 days for components, plus engine and sound work |
| 2 | Flat-vector cast: own puppet rig, 15-25 named actions, mouth driven by loudness, parallax sets, camera | Explainer-animation / stick-figure-channel grade, charming if art is consistent; weak walking, no real acting range | Same story structure with simple 2D characters, a different look | 10-15 days, 6-10 for rig and two characters alone |
| 2b | Skins on tier 2: paper-cutout at stepped 12 fps, doodle strokes | Cheap variety, forgiving look | none added | 3-5 days (cutout), 4-6 (doodle) |
| 3 | three.js toon-shaded rigged characters with outlines, camera rig, post effects | "Game cinematic from CC0 packs"; premium only with authored art | Closest in kind, still far in polish and sims | 25-40 days, high performance risk |

## Build text-and-stills formats first, then a flat 2D cast, and skip the licence traps

**Formats.** The market researcher recommends chat-style twist stories, "did you know" explainers with data inserts, and micro-lessons, and defers character stories as the hardest and most over-promised; the style researcher recommends motion comic, kinetic text and data/mockup scenes first, then a 2D cast. These reconcile into one build order: the tier 1 component set serves all of those formats at once, and the character stage follows as the first real "acting" tier. The format evidence is weak either way. The only concrete scale figure for an animated Hindi education channel is Curious Shorts at 2.24M subscribers (Oct 2024 data, not shown to be code-made) ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC2YOYJRD8wm6Eg6BtgqHtgw)), and no independent retention data for any code-made format exists, so every format ranking here is a hypothesis for the first 3-5 real users. One proposal of ours bridges the owner's ZackD ambition and what code can do: narrated true-story explainers drawn with icons, numbers, maps, timelines and props instead of human characters. It needs no character art and matches the channel's true-story titles, but factual accuracy and "fiction" labelling become product rules (the idea check must require a source field).

**Skip list.** Spine (each user needs a paid editor licence, so it cannot ship in an MIT app) ([Spine licence](https://eu.esotericsoftware.com/spine-runtimes-license)), Live2D (proprietary core and a publication contract), DragonBones (licence unconfirmed, editor reportedly unmaintained), Mixamo files (raw files cannot be redistributed, so a public MIT repo cannot hold them) ([Adobe FAQ](https://community.adobe.com/questions-696/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos-589400)), Ready Player Me (reported shut down after a Netflix acquisition, secondary sources only), LottieFiles free files (custom "same terms" licence), pixel art and shader-only landscapes as standalone styles, and GSAP. Rive is a poor core because the editor is proprietary, free exports carry a splash screen, and the AI can only drive the inputs an artist exposed ([Rive pricing](https://rive.app/pricing)); it can come later as an optional "import your own .riv". Stick-figure and quote-card formats are saturated or generic (vendor claims only), so treat stick-figure as a skin on the tier 2 rig, not a product.

**Character approach.** Build our own layered puppet rig in Canvas 2D, in `src/render`, drawn by the same `renderFrame`. The rig is a parts tree with parent transforms, springs, blink, breath, sway and gaze layers, and each character exposes parameters (`mouthShape`, `browL/R`, `eyeOpen`, `gazeX/Y`, `headTilt`, `armPose`) that the renderer computes from a beat list, so the AI never emits frame-level numbers. The acting vocabulary is a closed list that the characters researcher sketched as a design, not a finding: nine emotions with intensity (neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared, thinking, smug, excited), nine one-shot gestures (wave, point, shrug, thumbs_up, facepalm, clap, nod, shake_head, lean_in), four loop states (idle, talking, walking as slide-with-bob, holding a prop), six entrances and exits, five gaze targets, slot placement and six camera moves. A Zod enum validates it, and unknown names fall back to idle or neutral. The same rig can carry a monkey, cat, dog or farmer by swapping parts and palette; the notes found no confirmed CC0 talking monkey and found that 3D animal packs only have idle, walk, run, jump and death clips ([Quaternius animals](https://quaternius.itch.io/lowpoly-animated-animals)). Real walk cycles and leg IK are out of V1.

The mouth is loudness-driven: smooth the RMS envelope of the decoded voice buffer (attack about 40 ms, release about 80 ms) and map it to 3-4 mouth sprites, optionally adding a spectral hint for wide versus round. This is language-independent, so it works for English, Hindi and Nepali, and because export is offline the values must be precomputed from PCM rather than read live from an analyser. Phoneme tools are English-centred: Rhubarb offers 6-9 mouth shapes with an English-only default recogniser ([Rhubarb](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync)), its WebAssembly port weighs about 2.2 MB ([lip-sync-engine](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/lip-sync-engine@1.0.3/README.md)), and no source measured Hindi or Nepali accuracy for any lip-sync tool. A Devanagari vowel-to-mouth table is a possible later upgrade (researcher idea, untested). Lip-sync claims stay off the product page, as CLAUDE.md already requires.

**Art is the real tier 2 risk.** The cheapest low-risk route is art we author ourselves as code-drawn geometric paths, so no licence applies, with Open Peeps (CC0, hand-drawn layered people) or Humaaans (CC0 per its page) as optional starting parts ([Open Peeps](https://www.openpeeps.com/), [DiceBear licences](https://www.dicebear.com/licenses/)). For 3D, Kenney and KayKit are CC0 ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/support), [KayKit](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/kaykit-adventurers)), but Quaternius is contradicted: one note read its FAQ as CC0 ([FAQ](https://quaternius.com/faq.html)), while another read the licence page, last updated 28 Aug 2026, as a custom Quaternius Asset License that bars redistributing the assets as standalone packs ([licence page](https://quaternius.com/license.html)). Until the owner or Quaternius resolves it, treat Quaternius as not bundle-able. Whether a developer can draw a consistent character that audiences accept is unproven, so the first character task is a one-week spike with a pass/fail gate (G10 below).

## One engine, a compact AI spec and a validator stack keep the AI from breaking the frame

**Engine strategy (researcher recommendation, staged).** Keep Canvas 2D as the compositing spine and the text layer, because text already goes through the browser's native shaper, which is how the Devanagari fix works; any WebGL layer should consume text as a texture. Stage 0 adds no dependency: a `Layer` interface, a camera `{x, y, zoom, rotation, shake}` with per-layer parallax, a roughly 200-300 line keyframe and easing module (linear, cubic-bezier, Penner subset, closed-form spring, step, hold) with seeded noise for shake, a seeded PRNG for particles computed analytically from time (so any frame can be rendered alone), cheap 2D finishing (vignette, grain tile, tint), and a pixel-diff test of preview against export. GSAP is out: its licence is "proprietary free" with a clause against tools that help users build visual animations without code, which is a legal question for an AI video producer ([GSAP licence entry](https://open.windriver.com/info/uni-license-list/licenses/gsap-standard-no-charge-2025.html)); Theatre.js core is Apache-2.0 but Studio is AGPL and the 1.0 path is private ([Theatre.js](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre)). Stage 1 is richer 2D (puppets, parallax, particles). Stage 2, only when needed, is one hand-written WebGL2 post pass for colour grade, real bloom, depth of field and shader transitions, composited before captions. Stage 3, only if 3D is approved, is three.js (MIT, [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/LICENSE)) with `WebGLRenderer`, lazily imported and rendered into its own canvas. PixiJS, Babylon, Phaser and CanvasKit are not worth adopting (a second scene graph or about 1.5 MB or more of WASM per a Flutter benchmark). WebGPU stays optional: caniuse shows about 88% global support but a Firefox conflict between sources, and three.js falls back to WebGL2 on its own ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/webgpu), [three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer.html)).

**Determinism rules (engineering practice, each needs a test).** `renderFrame(project, tMs, assets)` stays a pure function: no `Date.now`, no `performance.now`, no `Math.random`, assets fully decoded before frame 0, DPR ignored, all pixel parameters (blur, grain, outline width) expressed in 1080x1920 units and scaled for preview, premultiplied alpha used consistently. For any WebGL canvas, call `source.add()` in the same task as the draw or copy into a 2D canvas first, because a canvas with `preserveDrawingBuffer: false` is cleared after the swap ([Chromium review](https://codereview.chromium.org/2627793003)). Mediabunny's `CanvasSource` takes explicit timestamps and gives backpressure from `add()`, which matches the existing export loop ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/main/src/media-source.ts)). A three.js export has a reported single-frame artifact bug with post-processing, partly reduced by `preserveDrawingBuffer` ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/random-single-frame-pixel-artifacts-in-webcodecs-export-from-effectcomposer-meshphysicalmaterial-transmission/91369)). Costs are unmeasured: the dev-machine figure of about 70 fps was for flat card scenes, and the arithmetic from the rendering note is that four-sample motion blur on that baseline would fall to about 17 fps. A comparative table shows 11 fps on a low-end Windows netbook against 200 fps on a MacBook M4 for 1080p30 H.264 encode alone, with no stated method ([webcodecsfundamentals](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/basics/encoder/)), so the owner's own weak PC is the real target.

**Spec design.** Every system that worked in the literature made the LLM a planner over a closed representation and let code own geometry: LayoutGPT used a CSS-like layout format ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.15393)), Holodeck's relational constraints beat absolute coordinates in a user study ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.09067)), SceneCraft's relation graph was the largest ablation effect (88.9 score with it, 3.2 without, on only 20 queries) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01248v1)), and a Vercel pattern pairs a Zod-validated catalogue with model-filled JSON ([InfoQ](https://infoq.com/news/2026/03/vercel-json-render)). Models writing raw Lottie JSON failed often in CVPR 2026 supplements ([OmniLottie](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Yang_OmniLottie_Generating_Vector_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)), and un-tuned 7-8B models often cannot produce executable code ([AutoPresent](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00912v2)), which supports the existing rule that the AI fills props and never writes code. No head-to-head test of a JSON spec against free-form code on the same video task exists.

The proposed design, all researcher proposals: a scene card is `{beat, say, use, p, cam, tr, sfx}` where `use` is one enum from a catalogue and `p` holds two to six defaulted props validated per component. Placement uses semantic anchors (left-third, center, ground, hero, top-safe, lower-third) and sizes (s, m, l, hero), never pixels. Time is bound to the narration line: the scene duration comes from the voice, the LLM gives only a weight (short, med, long), and animation offsets are `start`, `on-word:n` or `end`, never milliseconds. An empty `{use, p:{text}}` card must render acceptably, which makes repair trivial. The catalogue starts with 14 components: hook_text, big_number, image_focus, split_compare, bullet_reveal, quote_card, icon_row, bar_chart_simple, steps_timeline, character_stage, screen_demo, selfie_title, text_card (the universal fallback) and end_card, plus global layers (captions, backgrounds, camera enum, transitions, six sfx cues, six to eight theme packs). Variety comes from layering: a theme pack changes typography, palette, easing family and sfx together, a seed picks variants, and compiler rules forbid the same component three scenes in a row and require at least five distinct components in eight scenes.

The pipeline: the existing M3a script writer yields a beat sheet (free text); code binds user assets to beats and shortlists six to eight components per beat; one constrained call per beat returns a scene card; code runs Zod, normalisation, semantic checks and a bounded repair loop; the compiler resolves anchors, durations from voice, theme and seed; `renderFrame` draws it. A free-text beat sheet before constraint follows the finding that hard schemas can hurt creative accuracy in small models: one study saw schema validity rise from 61.5% to 100% while accuracy fell from 19.7% to 11.0% ([Constraint Tax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)), and another found constrained decoding fixes syntax but not instruction semantics ([small-model study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742)). XGrammar in WebLLM adds near-zero overhead but under-constrained complex schemas in one benchmark, so Zod always runs afterwards ([XGrammar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15100), [JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v2)). Provider adapters need care: Gemini honours enums and item limits but strips `maxLength`, may reject deep schemas, and orders keys alphabetically without `propertyOrdering` ([Gemini docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output)); Groq strict mode needs every field required and covers only some models, with conflicting model lists across sources ([Groq docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/structured-outputs)). On-device 1-4B models should emit only a four-field card (`use`, `text`, `sub`, `mood`) that the compiler expands, because their semantic failure rate is unmeasured.

The validator stack (all proposals): transport (provider structured output or JSON mode with a tolerant extractor), Zod per component, deterministic normalisation (clamp enums, shrink text, drop missing assets), code-computed checks (chars per line using `measureText` including Devanagari, safe area, box overlap, WCAG contrast, scene duration against audio, total 25-35 s, scene length 1.5-6 s, hook in the first 3 s, variety, asset coverage), a repair loop on the failing scene only with a numbered error list and at most two retries, then a rules fallback that maps beat role to a default component. Gains in agent loops plateau after about three iterations ([Planner-Actor-Critic](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05016)), and vision-model critics stay advisory-only because earlier research judged them unreliable. This fallback also fixes the current weakness that the basic brain makes an empty shot list: with scene cards it can produce a complete watchable video with no AI at all. Edits fit the existing ops model: a scene is the unit of regeneration, a user edit to any field path adds a lock, the AI patch is rejected in code if it touches a locked path, and "shuffle look" only bumps the seed, with no LLM call.

## A zero-cost sound stack: record or key a voice, align by energy, synthesise SFX and music, mix with ducking

**Voice.** The tiers per language are a researcher recommendation built on verified licences. English: the user's recording or upload; then Gemini TTS or Azure with the user's key; offline only as an experiment. Hindi: recording; then Gemini or Azure (nine standard neural voices plus HD); offline only through Supertonic 3. Nepali: recording; then Gemini or Azure's two Nepali voices, `ne-NP-HemkalaNeural` and `ne-NP-SagarNeural`; no offline option in V1 ([Azure voices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts), [Gemini speech](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation)). The offline English path is blocked by licensing. Kokoro's weights and code are Apache-2.0 and Hindi is listed, but kokoro-js depends on the `phonemizer` package, which embeds eSpeak NG, a GPL programme ([kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro), [kokoro.js package.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hexgrad/kokoro/main/kokoro.js/package.json), [eSpeak NG](https://www.freshports.org/audio/espeak-ng/)). Misaki, Kokoro's text-to-phoneme step, is Apache-2.0 and its English path does not need eSpeak, so a dictionary-only "permissive Kokoro" is possible but nobody has ported it to JavaScript, and out-of-dictionary words would be spelled letter by letter ([misaki](https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki)). Piper's current maintained line is GPL-3.0 ([piper1-gpl](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl)). Supertonic 3 covers English and Hindi in-browser but its weights are OpenRAIL-M (use-restricted, not OSI-approved) and the repository is archived ([Supertonic](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic)). Kokoro's Hindi voices are graded C on training data ([Kokoro voices](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)). Gemini's free tier is tiny (an anecdotal 10 requests per day; Google publishes no numbers) and unpaid use may be read by humans and used for improvement, so an on-screen notice is required ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). Gemini returns audio with no word timestamps.

**Captions and alignment.** The current caption timing is an estimate spread over the line. For a known script the researchers recommend aligning rather than transcribing: use Azure word-boundary events where available; otherwise detect speech and pauses in the decoded PCM, split phrases at pauses of about 150-250 ms, and distribute the known words by grapheme-cluster count (use `Intl.Segmenter` for Devanagari). A "refine" button can run Whisper through transformers.js with `return_timestamps: 'word'` ([pipelines docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/api/pipelines)), but no Hindi or Nepali word-timing accuracy exists in the sources, and the CTC aligner MMS-FA is non-commercial by memory, not verified. The few-hundred-millisecond error figure is an engineering estimate.

**SFX, music and mix.** Procedural SFX are the license-safe default: ZzFX is MIT, under 1 KB compressed, and each sound is a 20-number array, so 30-40 presets cost a few KB ([ZzFX](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel/ZzFX)). The AI picks a tagged vocabulary (whoosh_short, riser_tension, impact_hard, tick, pop, swell_pad, heartbeat and similar) plus time and intensity, and code resolves it to a preset. Music defaults to procedural mood presets (calm, tense, hopeful, epic, mysterious) from Web Audio or Tone.js (MIT) rendered in an `OfflineAudioContext` ([Tone.js](https://github.com/tonejs/Tone.js)); Strudel is AGPL-3.0 and excluded ([Strudel](https://strudel.cc/technical-manual/project-start/)). Bundled recordings should be CC0 only: Kenney is CC0, Freesound CC0 items need a login to download, and Sonniss bans redistribution as sound effects ([Freesound](https://freesound.org/help/faq/), [Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)). Kevin MacLeod is CC BY 4.0 with required credit and Content ID risk ([Incompetech](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/faq.html)), which is an owner decision. The mix chain is voice normalised near -16 dBFS RMS, music about -20 dB and ducked a further 8-12 dB while the voice is active (attack 80 ms, release 400 ms), SFX at -12 dB, a soft limiter, then a two-pass render with an in-house ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighting meter and one static gain to -14 LUFS with a -1 dB peak ceiling; libebur128 (MIT) is the reference for tests ([libebur128](https://github.com/martingasser/libebur128)). All of this is design reasoning, with no listening test or measured sound quality behind it, but the cited sources support the licences, and sound design is the researchers' strongest bet for a "premium" feel at zero render cost.

YouTube clarified in July 2026 its "inauthentic content" wording, with named categories of generic or repetitive videos, unsatisfying content and AI personas posing as experts, though Tubefilter says the rules did not change ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)), and a January 2026 sweep removed large AI-slop channels per vendor-sourced counts ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/)). A free tool that stamps identical looks is exactly the exposed pattern. YouTube's AI-use toggle exempts clearly non-realistic or animated content ([YouTube help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)), so flat 2D animation carries less labelling burden than realistic clips, but this should be re-read before launch. Monetisation is a poor success measure in Nepal: the 10M Shorts-views route to the Partner Programme is unrealistic for first-time creators, Facebook is the dominant platform, and Android holds about 75% of mobile share ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en), [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal), [Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/nepal)).

**How the existing milestones change.** M3 (brain) keeps M3a, which is built; M3b (craft cards, validator, Hindi/Nepali editor pass and eval harness from the script report) continues, and a new M3c is C3, so the brain's output moves from "shot list for external generators" to "scene cards". M4 (direction desk) shrinks to an optional later "hero shot" prompt pack and bring-your-own-key clip profiles. M5 (analyse and decide) is trimmed: rule-based asset-to-beat binding and the compiler's variety pass replace heavy analysis, and embedding-based matching waits. M6 (audio-first) becomes C1 and moves earlier. M7 (captions) is upgraded from estimated to aligned timing. M8 (sound) becomes C4 and moves earlier, because sound design is the cheapest premium signal. M9 (gap fillers, finishing, retention check) becomes C5's motion lint and variety guard. M10 and M11 are parked (D11). M12 (export) keeps its thin slice and still needs the AAC runtime check, `StreamTarget` writing, low-end benchmarks and resume-after-context-loss. M13 (ship) gains the licence audit of every bundled asset.

**Reuse.** Nearly the whole build base carries over, which is why tier 0-1 is weeks, not months. The shared `renderFrame` with Canvas 2D stays as the spine (C0 wraps it in layers). The text, number and end-card templates and their Zod props become three of the 14 catalogue components, so the "AI fills props, never writes code" rule is already in place. Stills with push-in become `image_focus` and the motion-comic base. Captions with bundled Inter and Noto Sans Devanagari, and the verified Hindi/Nepali rendering, are reused unchanged; text must stay on the Canvas 2D layer. Voice per line by recording, upload or Google TTS with the user's key stays as the voice base, with measured alignment replacing the estimate. Mediabunny export (1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264 plus AAC, about 70 fps on the dev machine for flat scenes) is the exporter, with the unmeasured risk being heavier character scenes. The Zod project JSON, ops and locks carry the AI-edit contract: add ops for `replaceScene`, theme and seed, and keep the AI's scope limited by locks. The story-craft research, hook cards and validators are the script stage that feeds the beat sheet, and the basic and AI brains become the rules fallback and LLM path. Because `src/model/` changes need approval from the other contributor when there are two, a single contributor should still record schema changes in STATUS.

## Conclusion

The reframing that matters is that "generate a clip by code" splits into two products with very different cost curves. The text, number, chart, chat and still-image tiers are an integration and polish job on assets that already exist, and they can ship with sound, aligned captions and a validated AI path in roughly 6 to 9 weeks. The character tier is an art problem wearing an engineering label: the rig, spec and mouth are tractable, but nobody can say whether a developer-drawn cast looks intentional until the one-week spike is judged by strangers. That makes the spike, not the 3D decision, the pivot of the plan, and it is cheap enough to run before committing weeks to tier 2.

The surprising consequence is that the sources point to sound and structure, not rendering, as the cheapest route toward the ZackD feel: escalating story beats, a camera and pop on every beat, designed sound and kinetic captions are code-reachable, while character appeal and lighting are not. A product that is honest about this, free of watermarks, Nepali and Hindi aware, and built on the owner's own craft cards and variety rules, competes on the axes the paid cloud tools do not serve, but only the first real users will show whether that is enough.
