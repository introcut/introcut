# Ship Code-Made Videos First, Defer Avatars

**Build V1 as a faceless story-and-explainer maker that also finishes the clips users bring, and park the photo avatar, the voice clone and the middleman business for V2.** Of the owner's four ideas, two are feasible now and two only in reduced form. Idea 2 (Introcut writes the script and draws the whole 30 s video by code, with free voice, music and sound effects) ships now, because the drawing and MP4 export engine already exists and measured about 70 frames per second on the dev machine (STATUS.md). Idea 3 (merge and curate 4-5 clips the user generated elsewhere) ships in reduced form: code can make clips match technically (size, speed, loudness, colour, frozen frames) but no open tool can reliably spot warped hands or garbled text, so the human picks. Idea 4 (channel management) ships as plan, make, package and track, with no auto-posting, because YouTube and TikTok lock uploads from unaudited apps to private and Meta needs app review. Idea 1 splits: a stylised presenter bubble driven by the voice's loudness ships; a realistic talking avatar from one photo does not, because every permissive photo-plus-audio model found needs 12-80 GB of graphics memory or a paid API at about $0.14-0.16 per second, and a clone of the user's voice is feasible only in English with a permissively licensed model (Chatterbox) as an optional companion. The web app stays the product (mobile is 65% of Nepal's traffic); a Windows app is an experiment, and the VoiceStudio repo is inspiration only (AGPL-3.0 app, non-commercial default voice weights). The "3 free then pay" idea cannot be enforced on browser export in an MIT app; only server-side features can be metered, and the honest V1 has nothing to meter and nothing to pay for, so payments wait for V2 while a one-day test of Dodo Payments (the only gateway that lists Nepal and accepts an individual) starts now. The full V1 plan is about 60-94 working days of effort (researchers' estimates), and eleven decisions belong to the owner (section 8).

How to read this report. "Evidence" means a fetched source with a link. "Proposal" means the researchers' or this report's own design or estimate. Several business notes were cut short by a tool usage limit, so their gaps are listed rather than filled; section 7 says which.

## Code draws every frame itself, so the result is clean, and free audio is made the same way

**A video is a stack of still pictures.** A 30 s clip at 30 frames per second (fps) is 900 pictures shown quickly. Introcut already has one function, `renderFrame`, that draws the picture for any moment from the project JSON file, and both the preview and the export call it (STATUS.md). The exporter loops through the 900 moments, hands each picture to the browser's WebCodecs encoder (a built-in compressor that uses the PC's video chip), and Mediabunny packs the result into an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC sound. That is why the output is clean: text is real text drawn by the browser (so the bundled Devanagari font renders Nepali correctly), shapes are exact, and nothing is guessed by a model. The evidence for why this matters is that in one study pixel video models scored near zero on text-heavy explainers, where code-based methods won clearly ([Code2Video](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01174)). The AI never writes code. It fills a small "scene card" chosen from a fixed catalogue (hook text, big number, chart, steps, chat story, screen demo and so on), and Zod validators, layout checks and a rules fallback keep a bad answer from breaking the frame (proposal from the earlier plan, building on the research that models do well as planners over a closed format and badly at raw code, [AutoPresent](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00912v2)). A user who gives no clips still gets a full video, which also fixes the weakness that the basic brain currently makes an empty shot list.

**What is measured and what is not.** On the dev machine a 27.2 s story exported in 11-12 s with software encoding, and a 14.8 s story with voices in 5 s (STATUS.md). Nobody has measured a weak laptop. One unsourced-method table shows 11 fps on a low-end Windows netbook against 200 fps on a MacBook M4 for 1080p30 H.264 encoding alone ([webcodecsfundamentals](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/basics/encoder/)), so a 30 s export could take from seconds to over a minute. The owner's own PC is therefore the first test (gate G9 in the earlier plan: at most 25 ms per frame at the 95th percentile and a 30 s export in 60 s or less, both proposals). WebCodecs reaches about 95.5% of global usage ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/webcodecs)), and AAC encoding is not guaranteed everywhere, so the runtime check and VP9/Opus fallback already in the exporter stay.

**Where code falls short, stated plainly.** Code cannot draw photoreal people or organic motion, and the ZackD.Films look is Blender work by contract 3D animators ([recruiting page](https://zackdfilms.recruitee.com/o/3d-generalist)). The earlier plan's conclusion stands: copy the structure (hook, escalation, twist, camera cuts every 1-3 s, sound design), own a flat 2D look, and treat a character cast as an art problem decided by a one-week spike (gate G10). The larger policy risk is sameness: YouTube's monetisation policy targets "repetitive or mass-produced" content and applies to the whole channel ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)), so variety rules (theme packs, seeded variants, no bulk mode) are part of the product, not polish.

**How the free audio is made.** Voice has three honest tiers. The user's own recording always works. The user's own Google (Gemini TTS) or Azure key adds Nepali and Hindi voices; Azure lists two Nepali voices ([Azure voices](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts)) and a free tier of 0.5 million characters a month ([Azure Speech](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/cognitive-services/speech-services/)), while Gemini's free tier is small and Google may use free-tier content to improve its products, so an on-screen notice is required ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). A free offline English voice is blocked: Kokoro is Apache-2.0 but its JavaScript package pulls in eSpeak NG, which is GPL ([kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro)), so the owner's earlier decision to skip it stands. Sound effects are procedural: ZzFX is MIT, under 1 KB, and each sound is a list of about 20 numbers ([ZzFX](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel/ZzFX)). Music is generated by presets in Tone.js (MIT), rendered offline ([Tone.js](https://github.com/tonejs/Tone.js)); bundled recordings must be CC0, and the user's own music is always allowed. The mix lowers music about 8-12 dB while the voice speaks ("ducking") and sets overall loudness near -14 LUFS (a perceptual loudness unit, "how loud it sounds" rather than how high the peak is). These mix numbers are proposals; the -14 target must be checked against each platform's current guidance. Captions use alignment of the known script against the audio rather than transcription, because Introcut wrote the words; no source measured Hindi or Nepali timing accuracy, so the caption timing test (median error at most 150 ms, proposal) is a gate. All of this costs the owner $0 per video.

## The photo avatar and voice clone need hardware or licences the target user does not have

**Avatar: the evidence.** The two best-known fast lip-sync models, MuseTalk and LatentSync, repaint the mouth of an existing talking video; they do not turn a still photo into video. MuseTalk is MIT, but its tested minimum, a 4 GB RTX 3050 Ti laptop, took about 5 minutes for 8 s of video, which is roughly 19 minutes for 30 s (the arithmetic is the researchers'), and its face region is only 256x256 ([MuseTalk](https://github.com/TMElyralab/MuseTalk)). The permissive models that do take one photo plus audio are video-diffusion models: Wan2.2-S2V is Apache-2.0 but needs at least 80 GB VRAM on a single GPU ([model card](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-S2V-14B)), and EchoMimicV3-Flash states 12 GB ([repo](https://github.com/antgroup/echomimic_v3)). Sonic is non-commercial ([repo](https://github.com/jixiaozhong/Sonic)), and InsightFace weights, which several "Apache" projects pull in, are non-commercial research only even when auto-downloaded ([InsightFace](https://github.com/deepinsight/insightface)). No project was found that runs a photo-plus-audio network in a browser, and no benchmark exists for Hindi or Nepali lip-sync on any model. Paid API routes exist at about $0.14-0.16 per second for OmniHuman ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman)), so $4.20-4.80 per 30 s (secondary prices). Free hosted GPUs do not cover this: Hugging Face ZeroGPU gives free accounts a few GPU minutes per day ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/spaces-zerogpu.md); the exact quota is forum-sourced), and Colab's FAQ bars serving a third-party app ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)). The hardware reality for the audience is that Nepal's traffic is 65.11% mobile and 34.4% desktop ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/nepal)), and the notes' guess that only 10-20% of Nepali Windows laptops have an entry NVIDIA card has no source.

**How to use the avatar feature anyway (the owner's question).** There are four honest routes, ordered from default to rare. First, a stylised presenter bubble: the user's photo in a circle over a screen recording or slides, with scale and bob driven by the voice's loudness, a blink timer and slow sway. This is language-independent (it uses loudness, not phonemes, so it works for Nepali and Hindi), costs nothing, and is plainly synthetic, which helps with platform labelling. A cut-out with a moving mouth is the same idea with a segmentation step and a risk of looking uncanny; no permissive cartoonising model was verified, so treat it as a spike behind the character-acceptance gate (proposal; the notes' design, [TalkingHead](https://github.com/met4citizen/TalkingHead) and BiRefNet are references, and the BiRefNet weights licence is not stated). Second, an avatar slot: the user makes a talking clip elsewhere (for example in Flow with reference photos, which the earlier plan gates as G7) and Introcut merges it like any other clip. Third, an optional companion for owners of 12-24 GB NVIDIA cards, using user-installed InfiniteTalk or MultiTalk (Apache-2.0, [InfiniteTalk](https://github.com/MeiGen-AI/InfiniteTalk)) through ComfyUI, with Introcut importing the output file. Fourth, a paid bring-your-own-key route with a cost preview and an on-screen notice. The honest on-screen line is: "This is an animated presenter, not a realistic video of you; mouth movement follows loudness, not exact lip shapes."

**Voice clone: the evidence.** No model found is permissive in code and weights, able to clone, and evidenced for Nepali. Chatterbox is MIT, covers Hindi among 23 languages but not Nepali, and stamps every output with a Perth watermark ([model card](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox), [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/master/README.md)). Only its English Nano (110M, "3x faster than realtime on 8 CPU cores") has a CPU speed claim; a 1.55 GB quantised multilingual browser build exists but its WebGPU speed and Hindi quality are unmeasured ([ONNX file list](https://huggingface.co/api/models/onnx-community/chatterbox-multilingual-ONNX/tree/main?recursive=true)). Hindi evidence is thin: open issues report poor output and one community adapter reports a character error rate of 0.1058 on 100 samples ([adapter card](https://huggingface.co/reenigne314/chatterbox-indic-lora)). OmniVoice has the strongest published Hindi and Nepali numbers (Hindi WER 4.33, Nepali FLEURS CER 3.43, self-built benchmarks) but its weights are CC-BY-NC, so it fails the hard rule and is useful only as a private listening reference ([model card](https://huggingface.co/k2-fsa/OmniVoice)). VoxCPM2 (Apache-2.0, about 8 GB VRAM) lists Hindi at WER 19.7 ([card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2)); svara beta (Apache card, 3.3B, Llama-3.2 base to verify) is the only permissive model claiming Nepali, with no published scores ([card](https://huggingface.co/kenpath/svara-tts-voiceclone-beta)). Hosted routes are paid or gated: ElevenLabs instant cloning starts at $6 a month and its blog lists Nepali ([pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing), [blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/voice-cloning-in-12-indian-languages-demonstrated-live-at-iit-delhi)); Azure custom voice is Limited Access; Google's instant custom voice is allow-listed ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/speech-service/text-to-speech/limited-access), [Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/chirp3-instant-custom-voice)).

**Voice clone: verdict and the consent flow it needs.** Offer English cloning first as an optional companion, after the consent system exists; run a 1-2 day Hindi bake-off on three volunteer voices (Chatterbox Multilingual-Hi, VoxCPM2, svara beta) before calling Hindi anything but experimental; say "no cloned Nepali voice yet; record your own or use a built-in voice". The consent flow is a proposal built on sourced pieces: show a random sentence plus a fixed consent statement, check by local speech recognition that it was read, compare the consent clip and the sample with an Apache-2.0 speaker model such as WeSpeaker ([card](https://huggingface.co/api/models/Wespeaker/wespeaker-voxceleb-resnet34)), accept only microphone-recorded samples, store a signed local record, and refuse a mismatch. It is a speed bump, not proof: a study found cloned voices changed speaker-verification results, so the honest controls are attestation, labelling and watermarking, with the MIT AudioSeal watermark as an Introcut-side mark ([study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02914), [AudioSeal](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/audioseal/main/README.md)). Labelling is also a legal direction, not a courtesy: YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic synthetic voices and likenesses ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content)); Instagram's "AI-generated profile" label, from 31 Aug 2026 per news coverage, limits reach for unlabelled profiles featuring an AI-generated person ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/instagram-renames-ai-creator-label-and-restricts-unlabeled-ai-generated-profiles/)); and India's IT Rules amendment, in force 20 Feb 2026, requires prominent labelling and persistent metadata where feasible, with the main duty on platforms ([Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content)). Nepal has no deepfake-specific law that the notes could find.

**What VoiceStudio can and cannot be used for.** The repo (debpalash/VoiceStudio) is real and active: AGPL-3.0, created 2026-04-09, pushed 2026-10-22, with one main developer and an Electron app plus Python backend ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/debpalash/VoiceStudio)). Its star counts conflict across sources, so ignore them as a trust signal. "646 languages" means the default OmniVoice model's training catalogue (Hindi 117 h, Nepali 171.5 h), not proven quality per language ([languages.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/main/docs/languages.md)). "Fully local" is largely true for the default path, with optional cloud features. It has no avatar or talking-head feature. Its own licence notice says the default model weights are CC-BY-NC and that "no production model currently has the complete evidence needed" for commercial use ([LICENSE-NOTICE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/main/LICENSE-NOTICE.md)); the voice tokenizer adds the Boson Higgs Audio 2 licence on top of Llama 3 terms ([licence](https://huggingface.co/k2-fsa/OmniVoice/raw/main/audio_tokenizer/LICENSE)). Install is about 10 GB, CPU generation is "often 10-50x slower than on a GPU", the benchmark table is empty, and field reports show generations aborting on 4-6 GB cards ([install](https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/blob/main/docs/install/windows.md), [performance](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/main/docs/performance.md)). Its consent lock is only provenance, and plain local synthesis never requires it ([profiles.py](https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/blob/main/backend/api/routers/profiles.py)). The verdict: inspiration only. Never copy its code (AGPL would force the whole build to AGPL), never bundle or require its models, and do not make it a default. Usable now only as a user-installed tool whose WAV the user imports. A localhost adapter is possible later but the server refuses cross-site requests unless the user sets an allowed-origins variable ([csrf.py](https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio/blob/main/backend/core/csrf.py)). The MIT app voicebox is the better licence match (MIT app, Chatterbox Hindi, Nepali not listed), though its individual engine licences are unchecked ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jamiepine/voicebox/main/README.md)). Borrow its ideas: the consent-lock data model, default-on watermark, and the per-language training-hours table for setting expectations.

## Merge-and-curate works because AI editors are mostly "an LLM writes the cut list, a normal renderer executes it"

**How AI editors work.** Public detail is thin and most are black boxes. The documented pattern is that transcript or frame descriptions go to an LLM or vision model, which returns an edit decision list (EDL, a list of cuts, order, captions) that an ordinary renderer executes. OpusClip describes clipping by prompts using visual, audio and sentiment cues ([Opus help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/how-to-use-clipanything.md)); Descript makes the transcript the editing surface and lets users pick the model behind its co-editor ([Descript help](https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/36803785502221-Underlord-beta-Your-AI-co-editor-in-Descript)); MoneyPrinterTurbo (MIT) is a documented open example: script, material, TTS, subtitles, music ([repo](https://github.com/harry0703/MoneyPrinterTurbo)). Introcut already matches this shape (script, shot list, project JSON, `renderFrame`) and the LLM never touches pixels. Because Introcut writes the script, whole stages that long-form clippers need (highlight finding, diarisation, virality scoring) are unnecessary: each clip is a known scene with a known prompt. What to borrow: MoneyPrinterTurbo's two caption-timing modes (use the TTS's own timing, align only when needed), and auto-editor-style loudness and motion thresholds ([auto-editor](https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor), Unlicense) for trimming dead frames and dead time in screen recordings. What to avoid: server-side Python stacks, stock-footage calls, and any project whose licence is unclear. No open project does "normalise, flag and rank user-supplied generated clips against a shot list", which is Introcut's gap to fill.

**What code can fix and what it cannot.** Technical mismatches are detectable and fixable in the browser: resolution, aspect and fps from container data; loudness; average colour and exposure (a bounded Reinhard-style transfer, 50-70% strength with a before/after toggle, is the proposal); speed (retime 0.8-1.25x, larger changes look wrong without interpolation); frozen, duplicated or black frames; and jarring hard cuts (crossfade or dip, J/L audio overlap). Semantic defects are different. In a May 2026 benchmark, multimodal models at spotting AI-video artifacts approached random performance ([Artifact-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18984)), and text-image similarity (CLIP) correlates only weakly with human preference and cannot see motion ([FETV](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.01813), [TC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08656)). So the take picker uses hard-fail filters first, then motion and flicker bands, and shows prompt similarity only as a tie-breaker; it never shows one opaque score, and a user's pick becomes a lock. The UI line is: "Introcut can make clips match technically. It cannot tell whether a hand looks wrong." No study measured how often free-tier 6-8 s clips have each defect, so the first test is 10 sets of 4 real free-tier clips judged by humans (the notes' suggestion).

**Continuity and watermarks.** Continuity tricks are start and end frames, reference images, and chaining the last frame of clip N into clip N+1. Flow supports start/end frames and ingredient references ([Flow help](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353334)); Kling documents start and end frames and advises keeping them similar ([Kling](https://kling.ai/quickstart/ai-video-start-end-frames)); free-tier availability of each is unverified. Introcut can extract the last frame in the browser for the user to upload, with no automation of any web page; chain at most 2-3 clips because drift accumulates (inference). Watermarks: Kling free output is watermarked and paid is not ([AI Tool Curator](https://www.aitoolcurator.com/learn/kling-guide/free-plan-limits/)), and Flow's visible-mark rule has changed (a toggle replaced the tier rule per third parties, so verify). Introcut detects and warns only; it never crops, blurs or covers a mark, picks framing by the subject, keeps a corner mark fully visible, never strips SynthID or C2PA data, and keeps the AI-generated reminder at export, as CLAUDE.md requires. A related prompt-side point: the free-tool prompt pack follows Google's own structure (cinematography, subject, action, context, style, then audio) and one action per clip ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1)). No evidence shows compiled prompts beat plain prompts on 2026 models, so that is a blind test to run with 20-30 creators before claiming it. "Viral prompts" are not a moat: the US Copyright Office says prompts alone do not make a user the author of the output ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/01/29/part-two-copyright-office-ai-report-says-creative-prompting-doesnt-constitute-authorship/)) and a prompt library is easy to copy.

## Channel management ships as plan, make, package and track, because posting APIs are gated

**Why no auto-posting in V1.** YouTube's `videos.insert` has a default quota of 100 uploads a day per project and extra quota needs an audit ([insert reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos/insert), [audit](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits)); videos uploaded by an unverified API service can be locked private with no appeal ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7300965)). TikTok says unaudited clients are restricted to private viewing ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-get-started)), with reported audits taking weeks. Instagram publishing serves professional accounts, Standard Access needs no review only for accounts the developer owns, and anything serving other people needs App Review with a privacy policy, screencast and per-permission justification ([Instagram docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review)). Facebook Reels can only be published to Pages, not profiles ([Reels API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/video-api/guides/reels-publishing)). Meta also needs the media at a public URL, which collides with "media never leaves the device", and refresh tokens need server storage (inference). No source says whether a Nepal-based individual can pass these reviews, so eligibility is unknown. The open-source scheduler Postiz confirms self-hosters must pass each platform's approval themselves and says it can take weeks, and it is AGPL, so it is a reference only ([Postiz](https://github.com/gitroomhq/postiz-app)).

**What V1 does instead (proposal).** A setup coach (one niche, one series format, a weekly slot, one or two platforms); an idea-to-script-to-video-to-posted board in local storage; export presets from the existing renderer (Facebook Reels: mp4, 9:16, 1080x1920, 24-60 fps, 3-90 s, H.264 or H.265, AAC 48 kHz; Shorts under 3 minutes, which is secondary-sourced); a per-platform text pack (title, description, hashtags, first comment, with limits to verify); a posting checklist with a link to each upload page; a "mark as posted" log with the URL; manual analytics entry; and .ics calendar reminders that work with the existing Google sign-in. Free schedulers already cover scheduling (Publer's free plan allows 3 accounts with 10 scheduled posts each, [Publer help](https://publer.com/help/en/article/what-is-included-in-publer-free-dliovh/); Buffer's is similar per a secondary comparison), so Introcut's differentiator is the beginner coaching and the package, not scheduling. A coach promises routine and feedback, not growth: the burnout data is real (52% of 1,000 US/UK creators reported burnout, [Cosmetics Design Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/07/17/52-of-content-creators-say-they-have-experienced-burnout)) but no source supports cadence rules or faceless-creator advice.

**Monetisation is slow, small and changing.** The owner's premise that Facebook pays creators is partly true: Nepali telecom news reported in September 2025 that Meta began monetising eligible Nepali accounts with a bank payout ([Nepali Telecom](https://nepalitelecom.com/facebook-monetization-in-nepal)), but thresholds in later reports conflict (5,000 followers and 60,000 watch minutes against 10,000 followers, 150,000 views and 3 reels) and Meta's official page could not be read ([Ekantipur](https://ekantipur.com/news/2025/12/18/en/has-facebook-monetization-been-shut-down-in-nepal-this-is-the-reality-00-18.html)). Nepal's central bank data shows Nepalis earned over Rs 3.53 billion from videos on YouTube, TikTok and Facebook in FY 2024/25 ([Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/index.php/news/nepalis-earn-over-rs-35-billion-from-youtube-tiktok-and-facebook-videos-81-47.html)); AdSense pays Nepal by check or wire ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14728152)); TikTok Creator Rewards lists no Nepal in third-party country lists and is unconfirmed officially; the only earnings survey found (2022) says 46% of full-time creators earn under $1,000 a year ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-economy-stats)). The honest message, per the notes: most beginners earn little or nothing for months, and Introcut cannot promise or speed up payment. Two product consequences: no bulk or near-duplicate mode, and exports carry no overlay watermark. On the latter, Instagram's head said reels with a creator's own logo are fine but it tries not to recommend reels carrying other apps' logos ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/), 2022), and TikTok's Direct Post rules forbid apps adding brand marks ([TikTok guidelines](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-sharing-guidelines)). A "made with Introcut" end card is therefore a reach risk to verify, not a safe paywall lever.

## Keep the web app as the product, test a Tauri Windows shell later, and skip the local helper

**Evidence.** Nepal browsing is 65.11% mobile, 34.4% desktop and 0.49% tablet in September 2026; among Windows desktops, 60.88% run Windows 11 and 39% Windows 10 ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/platform-market-share/desktop-mobile-tablet/nepal), [Windows versions](https://gs.statcounter.com/windows-version-market-share/desktop/nepal)). A Windows-only app drops the majority of traffic. Tauri v2 (Apache-2.0/MIT) wraps the existing Vite/React app with WebView2, which follows Edge Stable, so WebCodecs and OPFS should behave like Chrome; that is an inference, and nobody tested H.264/AAC encoding or Google sign-in inside it ([Tauri](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri), [WebView2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/concepts/distribution)). Code signing is a Nepal problem: Microsoft's Artifact Signing restricts individual Public Trust certificates to the US and Canada ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/artifact-signing/quickstart)). The realistic zero-cost routes are SignPath Foundation, which needs an OSI licence, manual approval per release and a published code-signing policy ([SignPath](https://signpath.org/terms)), and the Microsoft Store, with free individual registration but Nepal's eligibility unconfirmed ([Windows blog](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/09/10/free-developer-registration-for-individual-developers-on-microsoft-store/)); unsigned builds work but meet SmartScreen warnings. Native FFmpeg adds codec import and no tab memory cap, but an MIT app must use an LGPL build without libx264 and carry the notices ([FFmpeg legal](https://ffmpeg.org/legal.html)), and the web build's roughly 70 fps on flat scenes leaves little speed to gain (estimate). A localhost helper reuses the hosted UI but since Chrome 142 needs a Local Network Access permission prompt, has changed rules three times, and fails in Safari, so it is three moving parts for a solo developer ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/local-network-access)).

**Verdict.** The owner's concern that the web is hard for heavy AI is valid for avatars and cloning, but those are equally hard on desktop (CUDA, drivers, licences, multi-GB installs); a desktop app mostly removes memory and codec limits and does not make a photo avatar run on an 8 GB laptop with integrated graphics. Web is V1. The Windows shell is two small experiments after the scene engine works (proposal): a 1-2 day Tauri build with no sidecar that tests H.264/AAC encoding, OPFS, Google sign-in, install size and export speed on one Windows 10 and one Windows 11 low-end laptop, then a 3-5 day test of a downloaded LGPL FFmpeg sidecar against WebCodecs. Heavy AI companions stay "install it yourself, Introcut imports the file", the lowest-risk interop. Effort for a basic Tauri build is 3-6 days, rising to 2-4 weeks with FFmpeg, updates and signing (estimates).

## "3 free then pay" can only be enforced on server-side value, and Nepal's best payment route is Dodo Payments

**What can honestly be enforced.** Introcut is MIT and renders in the browser, so a modified or self-hosted copy can skip any local counter; no cryptography fixes that because the exporter runs on the user's machine (the backend note's design argument). Only things the server does (AI clips through a proxy, hosted voices, cloud sync) can be metered. The recommended default is therefore: keep local creation and export free forever, state that forks are unmetered, make the official build's paid value server-side, and promise "3 free AI-assisted creations" or a starting credit balance instead of "3 free videos". A design that spends the owner's money must stay behind a ledger: a Cloudflare Workers plus D1 service with Turnstile fits the zero-cost rule because the free tier hard-stops rather than billing (100,000 requests a day, 10 ms CPU per request, D1 100,000 writes a day) and the arithmetic for 10,000 monthly users fits inside it ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/), [D1](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/), [Turnstile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)). It conflicts with the repo's "no servers we pay for" rule only if the owner counts a free Cloudflare account as a server (decision D5). Abuse is real: a vendor-relayed Stripe figure says over one in six signups at AI companies links to multi-account abuse ([Adex](https://adex.com/blog/free-trial-abuse-saas)); if the free tier is browser-only, an abuser costs the owner $0.

**What costs money, and what does not.** Costs are zero for browser rendering, local scripts, clip merging, local channel planning and the stylised presenter. Text help is under one cent per video: the freemium note computes $0.0059 per script on Gemini 2.5 Flash and about $0.0075 for Gemini TTS. The real cost drivers are generated video and avatars. Veo 3.1 Fast lists $0.10-0.12 per second (30 s is $3.00-3.60) with no free tier, and Google's page says the Veo 3.1 models shut down on 22 October 2026, which is today, so prices must be a data table, not code ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Avatars via API are $4.20-4.80 per 30 s. Google Flow credits cannot be spent through the API (forum reply, [Google forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/does-google-one-ai-pro-include-api-access-for-veo-3-or-is-it-limited-to-flow-only/110794)), so Introcut still must not claim a user's Flow credits can be spent. The researchers' break-even arithmetic (all inputs assumptions: $50 fixed monthly cost, 1,000 signups, Paddle's 5% plus $0.50 fee) is: a browser-only free tier breaks even at about 1.27% conversion at $5 a month and 0.65% at $9, against a freemium median of 2.1% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)); a free AI story for every signup would need 30-52% conversion and is impossible; and a $5 plan can never include AI video. Rules that follow: never give free AI video, sell AI clips only as prepaid credits at 2-2.5x provider cost, cap any included allowance at 50% of net revenue.

**Why the middleman business is risky.** Three provider terms read in primary text forbid resale-like models: Luma requires written consent to "resell, sublicense, or otherwise make the Services, APIs, or Output available to third parties" ([Luma](https://lumalabs.ai/api/terms)); Replicate bars service-bureau use and sublicensing ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/terms)); fal.ai bars reselling a customer's rights and exposing its APIs directly to end users, as returned by a summariser ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/terms)). Google's Gemini API terms contemplate "API Clients" for end users and are silent on resale, so it is the most defensible upstream but unconfirmed, and the EEA requires paid services ([Gemini terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). Higgsfield shows aggregators can scale (reported $700M annualised revenue) but its founder says gross margins are well below SaaS norms, and its growth came with refund-style complaints and abuse problems ([teardown](https://www.productgrowth.blog/p/higgsfield-growth-teardown)). Hosted render APIs (Shotstack at 5-20 cents a rendered minute, JSON2Video, Creatomate) are unnecessary because Introcut renders in the browser; they use their own JSON schema, not Introcut's, and Shotstack bans reselling your rights ([Shotstack terms](https://shotstack.io/terms/)). The ranking by risk (reasoned, not sourced): sell the prompt and workflow layer, then bring-your-own-key, then capped credit packs under written provider consent, then shared-key credit resale last.

**Getting paid from Nepal.** Direct Stripe does not list Nepal and Polar's payouts exclude it ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/global), [Polar](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/supported-countries)); Lemon Squeezy depends on PayPal payouts and Nepal is not in its bank list ([Lemon Squeezy](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries)); PayPal's Nepal status is contradictory across blogs. Dodo Payments lists Nepal as a merchant and payout country and accepts an individual with a Nepali government ID verified by selfie, with review in about 1-3 business days after the site shows terms, privacy and refund pages ([countries](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/accepted-countries-and-territories.md), [verification](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/verification-process.md)). Its fees are 4% plus 40 cents, plus 1.5% international and 0.5% for subscriptions, with USD SWIFT payouts at $25 (the page contradicts itself on payout fees) ([Dodo pricing](https://dodopayments.com/pricing)); by the notes' arithmetic a $9 sale loses about 10% to fees, so sell prepaid packs of $15 or more. Paddle's customer-country list includes Nepal but its seller eligibility and individual onboarding are unverified ([Paddle](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle)); Stripe Atlas costs $500 once plus $100 a year and creates a US company with US compliance duties ([Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas)). Local gateways (eSewa, Khalti) onboard registered businesses, serve only Nepali wallets, show no subscription product, and one anecdote quotes NPR 30,000 plus 3% ([eSewa](https://blog.esewa.com.np/documents-required-esewa-merchant-api-integration), [Khalti](https://docs.khalti.com/)). Paying foreign providers is its own limit: the 2021 dollar-card rule allowed USD 500 a year online ([OnlineKhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/nepali-banks-can-issue-prepaid-dollar-cards-now-here-are-basics-you-need-to-know-about.html)), and a reported 2026 central-bank IT circular raises limits to USD 3,000-5,000 for IT entities ([B360](https://www.b360nepal.com/detail/28246/can-federation-welcomes-nrb-directive-to-expand-foreign-currency-access-for-ict-sector)). Tax claims (5% final tax on IT-export income, VAT thresholds) are secondary and conflicting; a Nepali accountant must confirm. Recommended route: personal PAN, a published site with terms, privacy and refund pages, a Dodo application, a written question to a Class A bank about how a USD payout is classified, a ward-office sole proprietorship later for local gateways and bank facilities, and Atlas only after traction and legal advice.

**Which business notes are partial.** The notes on render platforms, reseller terms, backend and metering, the prompt engine and payments all hit a usage limit. Unresearched: Runway, Kling business, MiniMax, BytePlus and DashScope terms; Creatomate's real prices; JSON2Video, Plainly and Rendi terms; Cloudflare Browser Rendering and GitHub Actions terms; HyperFrames, Hera, Jitter and Canva hosted options; Vercel, Netlify, Turso, Neon; Paddle's seller rules; chargeback and refund norms; privacy law beyond a short Nepal, GDPR and India summary; Google ID-token verification; Nepal willingness-to-pay. The freemium note was resumed and filled most of its gaps. These items are why this report recommends against any paid generation product in V1.

## Conclusion

The sources reverse the owner's order of ambition. The features that feel most magical to a shy beginner, a face that speaks and a voice that is theirs, are the ones the evidence says are hardest to deliver cleanly, legally and for free, while the least glamorous parts (drawing frames by code, matching clips, packaging posts) are already proven or cheap, and they happen to solve the same problem: the user never needs to appear or speak. The surprising consequence is that licensing and hardware, not model quality, decide the avatar and clone questions, and that law and platform labelling, not rendering, decide how safely they can ship.

The second finding is that the monetisation story and the product story pull apart. A free, local, MIT tool has little to meter, so charging early would mean inventing a server and spending money before the product has proved it can finish videos on a weak laptop. The honest V1 is therefore free, small and useful, it proves one thing (a first-time creator can go from an idea to a clean 30 s video and a ready-to-post package), and its measured results, from weak-PC export speed to blind ratings of the merged clips, are what will show whether V2 should be characters, voice cloning or paid generation. Until the first real users report back, every conversion, price and earnings figure in the notes is a hypothesis.
