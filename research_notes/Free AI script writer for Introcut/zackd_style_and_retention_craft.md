# ZackD.Films-style ~30 s narrative shorts and short-form retention craft: teachable rules for a script-writing AI

Research date: 2026-10-21. Method: about 16 web searches and fetches. Primary sources (YouTube/TikTok/Instagram help pages, the channel itself, TV Tropes, fan wikis) were mostly NOT directly readable. Several fetches returned 402/403, so for the channel I could see only search-engine summaries of fan wikis. Everything about the channel is therefore secondhand and low-to-medium confidence. I could not watch any video. No creator interview was found.

Labelling used below:
- "Cited Findings" are things a source says, with the source's reliability noted.
- "Inferences" include my own craft knowledge and reasoning. They are NOT evidence-backed unless stated, and they are the proposed rules for the LLM.
- Where a source is a vendor or marketing blog, I say so.

## 2. Retention mechanics of ~30 s shorts: what platform guidance and credible analyses support

### Takeaway
Every platform says watch behaviour (watch time/percentage viewed, and "stayed to watch" on YouTube) drives distribution, so the opening seconds matter, and everything else (loop, ending, text) is a means of keeping or recycling viewing time. Specific numbers (3-second hook rates, 80-90% benchmarks, "1.7-2.5 s decision time") come from vendor blogs, not platforms, and should be treated as folklore or directional.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube changed Shorts view counting from 31 March 2025: a view counts when a Short starts to play or replay, with no minimum watch time. The previous metric survives as "engaged views" (YouTube Analytics Advanced Mode), and Partner Program eligibility and earnings still rely on engaged views. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/404562/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-shorts-views.html)
- YouTube Studio shows a "Stayed to watch" metric (viewers who stayed vs swiped away). Comparing it to average retention shows rewatching, and retention above the stay rate suggests replays. (Creator-blog interpretation of YouTube Studio.) — [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost)
- Blogs commonly read a steep early drop as a hook problem and a late drop (last second or two) as an ending problem (unneeded outros, logos, blank space). Retention above 100% can occur on very short loops because of replays. No official universal "good retention" number exists. Compare against your own channel's similar Shorts. — [subscribr.ai](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-shorts-low-watch-time-boost), [subscribr.ai perfect Short](https://subscribr.ai/p/perfect-youtube-short-length-structure-hooks)
- Bytecap (an evidence-synthesis page, with no measured stats and an explicit statement that it is not a customer-performance study) says YouTube's Shorts ranking considers chose-to-view, average view duration, average percentage viewed, likes and satisfaction signals, and cites YouTube support pages I could not read. It recommends changing one hook element at a time and judging hooks by chose-to-view and the opening of the retention curve. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts)
- TikTok: a blog attributes to the TikTok Creator Portal the line that content with strong average watch time and completion rate is more likely to be shown to a broader audience. I did not verify this on TikTok's own site. — [teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-video-be), [opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hook-formulas)
- "Hook rate" is the share of viewers who watch at least the first 3 seconds. Claims of 80-90% hook retention as "strong", "33% scroll past in 3 s", and a "21-34 s TikTok sweet spot" all come from vendor/marketing blogs and conflict with sources that say benchmarks vary and recommend using your own median. — [opus.pro](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hook-formulas), [nestscale](https://nestscale.com/?p=47200), [teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-video-be)
- Instagram Reels: coverage (secondary, marketing blogs) reports that Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the main ranking signals in 2025. Watch time is top, likes matter more for existing followers, and sends matter more for reaching non-followers. Claims of exact weightings (sends 3-5x likes) and "original content earns 40-60% more distribution" could not be verified. A blog also says Instagram will prioritise "raw, real human" content over AI-generated material in 2026 and warns against reposting watermarked content from other platforms; I could not confirm this in a primary source. — [stackinfluence](https://stackinfluence.com/how-does-the-algorithm-for-reels-work-in-2025/), [contentgrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/instagram-influencer-marketing/), [fanpagekarma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/?p=9293)
- Hook decision time: a 2026 vendor guide gives average keep-or-skip decision times of 1.7 s (TikTok), 2.1 s (Reels) and 2.5 s (Shorts). A vendor report claims question-style openings gave a 22% lift in retention through the first 5 s. Both are unverified vendor numbers, not platform data. — [gofaceless.ai hook guide](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook)
- Hook components: the first visual, the spoken line, the caption and the promise together make the hook. Test hook types on chose-to-view and early retention rather than assuming a universal winner. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts), [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook)
- Open loops: defined as a gap between what the viewer knows and wants to know, justified by the Zeigarnik effect (a 1920s finding). The "90% better recall" figure is a popular claim I could not verify. Advice: open the loop in the first ~3 s, close it last, never answer early, and end within seconds of the payoff. — [socialync.io](https://www.socialync.io/blog/open-loops-in-content-2026), [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook)
- Hook taxonomy (Bytecap's own, unmeasured): result-first, precise question, problem-solution, story teaser, surprising claim, visual reveal, direct promise. Each has a stated risk, e.g. result-first gives away everything; story teaser withholds the payoff too long; a surprising claim can overpromise. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts)
- Retention-curve diagnosis advice: early leave means the hook is unclear; leaving before the main point means pacing is slow. — [teleprompter.com](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-tiktok-video-be)

### Inferences
- Folklore vs evidence: SUPPORTED (consistent across platform-derived statements): watch time/completion and early retention influence distribution; Instagram uses sends and likes too. FOLKLORE or vendor numbers: exact 3-second thresholds, 80-90% benchmarks, "ideal length" claims, Zeigarnik percentages, original-content percentage boosts.
- Design implications that hold regardless of the numbers: (a) put the whole hook (first frame, first sentence, on-screen caption) into the first ~2-3 s; (b) make a 30 s short that feels like 20 s: no setup throat-clearing, no outro; (c) end immediately after the payoff, since a dead tail is a documented retention leak; (d) a loop or rewatch ending can lift average percentage viewed above 100% on very short pieces, but this is an optimisation, not a guarantee; (e) engineer for sends ("this is exactly like our village/family") on Reels.
- No tactic guarantees virality. The script writer should say it increases the odds of retention and should never promise outcomes.

### Gaps
- Official YouTube, TikTok and Instagram help pages could not be fetched. Everything above is relayed through secondary sources and should be re-verified before being quoted in Introcut docs.
- No peer-reviewed study of 30-second narrative-short retention was found.
- No data on how AI-generated visuals specifically affect retention. Instagram's stance on AI content is only reported secondhand.

## 3. Proven frameworks that map to ~30 seconds, with beat sheets

### Takeaway
The consistent public advice (hook, one tension, escalation, twist or reveal, payoff, stop) maps well to Zack-D-like shorts. The concrete second-by-second beat sheets below are my own synthesis from standard micro-fiction and short-form craft, not measured data, and must be labelled as defaults to be A/B-tested.

### Cited Findings
- Sources agree on: open the hook immediately, create a gap (open loop), deliver the promised payoff, and end quickly after it. — [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook), [socialync.io](https://www.socialync.io/blog/open-loops-in-content-2026)
- One guide says each beat should either advance the idea or reset attention. — [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook)
- A replication guide recommends hook, 6-10 short beats, twist ending, 20-45 s. This is its own advice and not confirmed to match the channel. — [longstories.ai](https://longstories.ai/blog/complete-guide-ai-3d-animation-youtube)
- Channel format per fan wikis: curiosity premise, then "seems harmless but..." escalation to a disaster or reveal, fact or fiction or thought experiment. — [Fandom wiki](https://zackdfilms.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Shorts)
- Bytecap's hook taxonomy (above) supplies seven opening patterns. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts)

### Inferences (my own proposed beat sheets; speaking pace about 2.4-2.5 words per second)
**Universal skeleton: HOOK (promise or question or shocking claim) > STAKES/SETUP (one fact) > ESCALATION (1-3 beats, each raises tension or surprise) > TWIST/REVEAL > PAYOFF (meaning, humour or emotion) > STOP (optionally echo the hook for a loop).**

**15 s (about 35-40 words, 1 twist)**
| Time | Beat | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Hook: question or odd claim | 6-8 |
| 3-9 | Surprising explanation (the answer) | 14-16 |
| 9-13 | Consequence or "so what" | 10 |
| 13-15 | Button line (punch or callback), then stop | 5-8 |

**30 s (about 70-78 words)**
| Time | Beat | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | HOOK: premise that raises a question (a visual plus one sentence of 6-9 words) | 6-9 |
| 3-8 | SETUP/STAKES: who, what is at risk, with one concrete number or object | 10-12 |
| 8-18 | ESCALATION: 2-3 beats of rising tension or strangeness; one visual change per sentence | 22-26 |
| 18-25 | TWIST/REVEAL: the surprising cause, answer or inversion | 15-18 |
| 25-30 | PAYOFF + STOP: meaning, emotion or joke; optional callback to hook word for a loop | 10-14 |

**45-60 s (about 110-150 words; 2 open loops)**
| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Hook |
| 3-10 | Setup, stakes |
| 10-25 | Escalation 1 (mini-reveal that opens a second question) |
| 25-40 | Escalation 2 (complication or reversal) |
| 40-50 | Main reveal |
| 50-58 | Payoff and meaning |
| 58-60 | Callback or final button |

**Framework-to-genre map (my synthesis)**
- Hook-Problem-Twist-Payoff: the default for fiction and fact stories.
- "What if" thought experiment: hook is the hypothetical, then 2-3 consequence steps that each get stranger, then a human-scale consequence.
- Mystery/Reveal: show the effect first (flattened field), hide the cause until about 70% of the runtime.
- Before/After: contrast statement in the hook, then the one change that explains the difference.
- Micro-horror: normal detail, then one wrong detail, then implication, then cut to black on the last line. Never explain the monster.
- List ("3 signs..."): numbered items, with the strongest or most surprising item LAST. Keep about 7 s per item. Promise the count in the hook.
- Myth-bust or "seems harmless, but": hook states the harmless fact, escalation reveals the damage (matches the channel's reported pattern).

### Gaps
- No public source gives second-by-second beat timing for Zack D. specifically, and I could not verify the timings against retention data. Treat the tables as starting defaults for A/B testing.

## 4. Narration/copy rules an LLM can follow and an evaluator can score

### Takeaway
Plan on 130-150 wpm (about 2.2-2.5 words per second), so 30 s means about 65-75 words, with room for pauses. The remaining rules (sentence length, no filler opener, one idea per sentence, concrete nouns) are craft conventions I propose as scoreable checks. They are not backed by platform research.

### Cited Findings
- 150 wpm is the common baseline for conversational narration. Standard voiceover pace is about 140-160 wpm. 140 wpm is a benchmark leaving room for graphics. Scripted narration often runs about 130 wpm. Energetic ads run 160-180 wpm. One YouTube script analysis found a median of about 166 wpm in its sample. — [voices.com](https://www.voices.com/tools/words_to_time_conversion), [rows.com](https://rows.com/calculators/video-script-calculator), [voicecontrol.pro](https://voicecontrol.pro/blog/words-to-time/), [irpr sound design](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/tools/voiceover-word-count-calculator/), [overseeros.com](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-script-length-word-count)
- Arithmetic from those sources: 30 s equals about 65 words at 130 wpm, 70 at 140 wpm, 75 at 150 wpm, and about 85 at 170 wpm. (My arithmetic, not a source claim.) — same sources as above
- Hook components include caption and first line, and the promise must be delivered. — [gofaceless.ai](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/how-to-write-a-video-hook)
- The channel's reported voice: a single narrator, talking in a lighthearted, informative tone, with the mood of the voice shifting by topic. (Fan wiki; low reliability.) — [YouTube Fandom wiki](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films), [shapes.inc](https://shapes.inc/zackdfilms/fun-facts)

### Inferences (proposed checklist; each item is machine-checkable)
Hard constraints (pass/fail):
1. Word count: target 2.4 words per second, tolerance +/-8% (30 s: 66-78 words, 15 s: 33-39, 60 s: 135-155). For Hindi/Nepali, re-calibrate with TTS timing, since syllable counts differ (unverified, no source found).
2. The first sentence is at most 9 words and contains a concrete noun or number or a question. It does NOT start with filler ("Did you know", "Hey guys", "In this video", "Have you ever wondered"), and it creates an open loop within 3 s.
3. Average sentence length is 6-12 words, and no sentence exceeds 18 words.
4. One idea per sentence. Do not join two claims with "and" or a comma splice.
5. At least one concrete, sensory, specific noun per sentence (rice, torch, well, handset). Penalise abstract nouns ("situation", "issue", "journey").
6. Stakes within 8 s: who loses what if it goes wrong.
7. The loop opened in the hook is closed in the last 20% and not earlier.
8. The last sentence is at most 14 words, delivers payoff, and has no CTA filler like "like and subscribe" inside the story body. If a CTA is wanted, keep it to one short line AFTER the payoff, or use a loop ending instead.
9. Person/tense: present or simple past, active voice, third person for fictional characters, second person ("you") only in the hook or the final beat for relatability. Consistent tense throughout.
10. Every sentence maps to a visual beat of 2-4 s (cut rhythm: a new visual about every 2-3 s, so about 10-14 visuals in 30 s). No sentence needs on-screen explanation the visual cannot show.
11. Fact claims are checkable or flagged (see honesty rules). Fiction is labelled "(fiction)" as the channel reportedly does.
12. No fake precision, no virality promises.

Soft quality scores (0-5 each): hook strength; escalation (each beat raises stakes or surprise); twist clarity; emotional resonance; rewatch or loop value; voice consistency; cultural specificity (proper nouns, objects, foods, units); avoid clichés and AI-isms ("In a world where...", "little did he know", "a testament to", stacked rule-of-three).

Delivery/audio guidance (craft, unverified): a short beat of silence or music change before the twist; keep music under the voice; no long pause at the end because blank tails are cited as a retention leak.

### Gaps
- No source on Hindi or Nepali speaking rates. I found none and will not guess. Measure from the chosen TTS output.
- No empirical source on ideal sentence length or cut rate for 30 s shorts. Those numbers are craft convention.

## 5. Relatability for South Asian / Nepali / Hindi audiences (farming and everyday life)

### Takeaway
Public evidence is thin. There is no study of Nepali or Hindi short-video preferences. Weak sources point to family, rural livelihood, moral dilemma and daily-routine themes. The rest is craft advice, framed as hypotheses to test.

### Cited Findings
- A 2024 study of rural South Indian creators (IJWBC) found villagers gained online followings by posting short videos of daily rural life, and some went viral. It covers South India, not Hindi-speaking areas or Nepal. — [Inderscience: YouTube and the production of online video cultures in Rural South India](https://inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=136669)
- A commercial analytics page for a Hindi animated storytelling channel reports its audience as mostly women and families in rural and semi-urban India (geographic split: India 85%, Pakistan 8%, Nepal 4%, Bangladesh 3%) and says viewers respond to traditional domestic dynamics, local village livelihoods and moral dilemmas. Tool-estimated, reliability uncertain. — [OutlierKit channel analysis](https://outlierkit.com/channel/naitikkahaniya-hindi)
- A general overview of rural-India "dehati" videos says family is a central theme, and many videos document farmers' routines, challenges and innovative techniques. Weak, blog-grade. — [Exploring Rural India: Dehati Videos](https://digitalocean.wpcompress.com/blog/exploring-rural-india-a-look)
- A trend piece claims regional-language videos (Tamil, Telugu, Bengali) often outperform Hindi or English in their regions. No source given; weak. — [search result summary of an unnamed trend article](https://monitorbp.ne.gov/amber/latest-insights-on-top-indian-mms-videos-updates-and-trends.html)
- Channel pattern (fan sources): the Zack D. channel itself relies on everyday-life, body, history and odd-fact topics plus recurring cartoon characters. This is evidence that "everyday" topics work for this format, not evidence about South Asia. — [YouTube Fandom wiki](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Zack_D._Films)

### Inferences (craft hypotheses, not evidence)
- Specificity beats generic "farmer": named village or district (Chitwan, Jhapa, Bihar, Vidarbha), a real crop (paddy, millet, mustard, tomato), real objects (doko, hasiya or sickle, bullock, tractor), real stressors (monsoon timing, loan or mahajan, middleman price, migration of sons abroad for work, remittance), and local festivals (Dashain, Tihar, Chhath, Holi).
- Recognition moments make people send or share: a line that makes the viewer think "that is exactly my mother or father or village". Write at least one "I know that feeling" detail per script.
- Cultural story patterns worth encoding (hypotheses, validate with local readers): the moral-turn or katha ending (a short parable that ends with a lesson), family duty versus personal ambition, the humble underdog vindicated, elders' practical wisdom ("jugaad" or low-cost ingenuity), sacrifice expressed through food and care rather than words, and gentle humour about daily frustrations (load-shedding, mobile network, the neighbour).
- Language: the writer should support Hindi, Nepali (Devanagari) and simple English. Prefer short, spoken-register sentences. Avoid translating idioms literally and keep honorific levels consistent (aap/timi/tapai). The LLM should draft natively in the target language, not translate from English.
- Respect and safety: avoid stereotyping caste, religion or poverty. Present farmers as competent problem-solvers, not victims only. Do not use real people's faces or voices.
- For farming content, flag agronomic claims as "verify with local extension office" because fact errors destroy trust. A script writer must not invent statistics.

### Gaps
- No Nepali-specific audience, language or culture study found. No Hindi short-video preference survey found. The OutlierKit audience estimate is a single commercial tool's estimate.
- Next searches that could fill this: Nepal digital media or NTA surveys, Nepali YouTube creator interviews, and Kantar or Meta reports on Indian short-video behaviour.

## 6. Original illustration scripts (written by me; illustrations of STRUCTURE only, not copied from any channel)

### Takeaway
Five original scripts in the proposed structure, each with beat timings and word counts. All fictional or illustrative. Any real-world facts (marigold and nematodes, millet water use, soil signs) are well-known generalities and must be verified before real use.

### Cited Findings
- None. These are my own examples. Word counts are my counts, calculated to land near 2.4-2.7 words per second.

### Inferences / The scripts

**Script 1: Mystery/Reveal, 30 s, (fiction), 73 words**
- 0-3 HOOK: "Every night, Ram's rice field was flattened." (7)
- 3-8 STAKES: "No footprints. No broken fence. Harvest was two weeks away." (10)
- 8-18 ESCALATION: "So he waited with a torch. At midnight, the ground trembled. Something heavy was breathing, walking straight toward the rice." (20)
- 18-25 TWIST: "The torchlight caught it. A starving young rhino, limping, hunting for grass after the flood." (15)
